# [HALO Mars](https://halomars.pages.dev/)

Up to 22 light-minutes from Earth, with no GPS or emergency extraction,
Martian astronauts need to be completely self-sufficient to survive.

**HALO Mars (Hazard-Aware Layered Operations on Mars)** is an
interactive, real-time 3D navigation tool that answers one critical
question: **Can the crew still get home?**

Astronauts cannot rely on geographic distance alone to estimate how far
they can explore from base. The walk back can cost far more oxygen and
energy than the trek out once steep climbs, loose sand, sample weight,
headwinds, and solar storms are included. They need a multidimensional
approach to identify the weakest link before their safety envelope is
breached.

HALO Mars fuses five NASA datasets: HiRISE/MOLA slope, THEMIS sand and
cold, CRISM sample targets, MEDA wind, and RAD radiation; with DONKI
solar storm alerts, alongside the astronaut's heart rate, payload mass,
and remaining oxygen, battery, and calories.

Its dual-pass route solver scores the outbound and return costs on the
terrain grid, then paints a live **Point of Safe Return**: a green safe
zone, an amber caution band, and a red turn-back region. A 360° horizon
skyline matcher provides a GPS-free position approximation on Mars.

**HALO Mars** is also our nod to saying *"Hello, Mars!"* As humans, when
we greet a new world and make it home, we have to make sure every
explorer has a protective halo on each part of this journey.
